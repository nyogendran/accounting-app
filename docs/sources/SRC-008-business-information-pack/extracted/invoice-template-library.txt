TIQACC · DELIVERED TEMPLATE LIBRARY
Invoice and document
templates
The layouts the system is delivered with. Every entity may issue on the delivered template as it stands,
brand it, amend it, or replace it with its own — and may keep as many saved templates as it needs, one
per country, per entity, per document type or per customer.
No template decides which taxes it shows. The taxes activated for the entity, the country and the supply
decide, and the template renders whatever they produce — each tax on its own line, in the calculation,
posted to its own account. The last page sets out those rules, which apply to every template here and to
every template an entity builds.
Each page below carries the layout, the fields that are mandatory on it, and the rules the build must
enforce. Codes in the left column are the template identifiers to load.

WHAT THE BUILD MUST ENFORCE ON THIS TEMPLATE
Title printed exactly as TAX INVOICE, bold and boxed, in a conspicuous position, and the serial number as YYMMM_QQQQ_XXXXX — at most 40 characters, no
spaces. Neither is configurable; the field map carries the full numbering rule.
Supplier block top left, purchaser block top right. TIN is nine digits for both parties, validated on entry, and the invoice cannot be issued without both.
Both dates print as MM/DD/YYYY — the order the gazette specifies, not the Sri Lankan convention, so it is fixed here and never taken from the locale. Amounts in
LKR to two decimals, with the three prescribed rows in the gazette's order and wording.
Any levy the entity's industry attracts prints above the value of supply, so that figure already includes it and the prescribed VAT row still reads as the gazette
intends. Which levy, at what rate and on what base comes from the industry tax set on page IND-TAX, not the template.
A tax invoice carries only supplies subject to VAT; a mixed order is split and SL-NTX-01 issued for the remainder (gazette cl. 4.2). Remittance details print in the
footer under cl. 6.3, drawn from the entity bank account that matches the invoice currency.

WHAT BRANDING MAY AND MAY NOT CHANGE
May change: logo and trademark in header or footer, typeface, colour, block order, and added columns or rows such as HS code and discount — gazette cl. 6.2 and
6.3.
May not change: the title wording, the presence of either TIN, the serial number format, the date format, the three prescribed total rows and their order, or the
exclusion of non-taxable lines.
Industry levies are not a template change. Setting the entity's industry under 2.1.7 activates that industry's tax set, and every levy in it appears above the value of
supply at its own rate and base. A levy that does not apply prints no line.
The designer validates a branded template against the mandatory field set before it can be saved as active. A template failing validation saves as a draft and cannot
be issued against.
Where the Commissioner General amends the format, the delivered template is reissued as a new version and derived templates are flagged for re-validation;
issued invoices keep the version they were produced under.

HOW TAXES AND CURRENCY BEHAVE ON ANY TEMPLATE
Every tax type activated for the supply prints as its own line, in the configured order, with its own label, its own rate and its own base. Activating a tax adds the line
and includes it in the calculation; no template is redesigned and no code is changed.
Each tax states the base it was charged on, because the bases differ: a levy may be charged on the value of supply while VAT is charged on the value plus that levy.
The order of application is configuration, not something a user applies by hand.
A tax that does not apply to a line shows a dash and is excluded from its base, so one invoice may carry three taxes on one line and one on the next. A fixed-
amount tax, such as a stamp duty, prints on the same footing as a percentage one.
Where the invoice currency is not the entity functional currency, the rate, its source and its date print, together with the total and each tax amount in the
functional currency, because tax is declared and paid in that currency. The block is suppressed when the two currencies match.
Taxes withheld by the customer are a memorandum beneath the total, never a line in it, and the bank account printed is the one held in the invoice currency.
Each tax line carries the account it posts to, so the printed line and the ledger entry come from one configuration, and the permitted tax set is configured per
country.

WHAT SEPARATES THIS FROM A TAX INVOICE
The title is INVOICE, never TAX INVOICE, and the document carries an explicit statement that no VAT has been charged.
It runs on its own number sequence, separate from the statutory tax invoice series, so the tax invoice series stays gapless and auditable on its own
(specification 2.1.10).
No VAT column, no VAT total row, and no VAT rate anywhere on the face of the document.
The reason tax is not charged is mandatory and is selected from the entity tax register — exempt, zero-rated, outside scope, or supplier not registered —
not typed free-hand.
Where a single order mixes taxable and non-taxable lines, the system issues a tax invoice for the taxable lines and this document for the rest, cross-
referencing each to the other.
Dates print in the entity locale here, because no gazette fixes the format for a non-tax document.

BUILD RULES
Two variants of this document exist and the system chooses between them from the original: a tax note where the document being adjusted carried tax,
and a non-tax note, carrying no tax line at all, where it did not. The same split applies in every country.
Each variant draws on its own gapless sequence, so tax notes and non-tax notes are never numbered from the same series (specification 2.1.10).
Every tax the original document charged is reversed or raised on its own line, at the rate and on the base that applied then — not at today's rate. Where
the original carried three taxes, so does the note.
A partial adjustment carries the taxes proportionately, each recalculated on its own base rather than apportioned from a single total.
The tax credit note reverses every tax the original document charged, each on its own line, in the period the note is issued rather than the period of the
original invoice.
Carries the number and the date of the document it adjusts. It cannot be issued without a valid reference to an issued document.
Where the original was in a foreign currency, the note is issued in that currency and the exchange rate block prints as it did on the original, so the tax
reversed in the functional currency agrees with the tax originally charged.
The reason is selected from a configurable list and is mandatory. Free text may be added but may not replace the coded reason.
Where the adjustment exceeds a configurable threshold or the original invoice is in a closed period, approval is required before issue (specification 2.3.9).

BUILD RULES
Both VAT numbers are mandatory for a cross-border business supply, and the customer number is validated against VIES at the point of issue, with the validation
response stored as evidence.
A separate net and VAT subtotal is printed for each rate applied — article 226 requires the breakdown, not just a single VAT total.
Sequential numbering that uniquely identifies the invoice is a directive requirement, so the series is gapless and a cancellation leaves a cancelled document, not a
missing number. Where a SEPA mandate is held, the mandate reference and collection date print with the bank block.

automatically; it is not typed by the user.
Where the entity is registered in more than one member state, the VAT number and the bank account printed are those for the state of supply, not the entity
default.
Country variations — Italy's SdI, Poland's KSeF, France's e-invoicing mandate — are handled as e-invoicing transmissions against this same template, not as
separate layouts.

HOW AN INDUSTRY TAX SET BEHAVES
A tax set is delivered per industry per country. Selecting the industry at entity set-up activates it; the levies then appear on every document they cover, in the
calculation, in the ledger against their own accounts and in the returns that report them.
An entity operating in more than one industry carries more than one set. The levy that applies is resolved per line from the item or service class, not from the
entity, so one invoice can carry a telecommunications levy on one line and none on the next.
Each levy states its own base. A levy may be charged on the net value while the transaction tax is charged on the net value plus that levy, and a second levy
may be charged on neither. The order of application is configuration and the printed line names the base it used.
A levy may be rate-based, per unit, banded, capped or a fixed amount, and may be charged on turnover, on a narrower class of supplies, or on a value that
includes another levy.
A sector levy introduced, changed or withdrawn by an authority is a dated configuration change. Documents issued before the effective date keep what they
were issued with, and a credit note against one of them reverses at the levy and rate that applied then.
Where a levy is not charged per invoice but computed periodically on a different base — the financial services case above — it is configured as a periodic tax
computed from the ledger, and nothing appears on the sales document.
Where an authority restricts what may appear on its prescribed document, the designer refuses a levy line it does not allow and the levy is carried inside the
value of supply instead.

CURRENCY, EXCHANGE RATE, AND A TAX THAT DID NOT EXIST YESTERDAY
Where the document currency differs from the entity functional currency, the exchange rate, its source and its date print on the document, together with the
total and each tax amount in the functional currency, because tax is declared and paid in that currency. The block is suppressed entirely when the two match.
Where a country requires the rate from a named source, that source is configured per country and the rate is taken from it rather than from the entity default
(specification 2.2.9). A third currency may be shown alongside without affecting the posting.
A new tax introduced by an authority is set up as configuration: name, mechanic, rate, base, order of application, the supplies it covers, its account, its
effective date and the documents it prints on. From that date it appears on every document it covers and in every return that reports it, with no template
rebuilt and no release required.
A rate change is a dated change to the same tax, so documents issued before it keep the rate they were issued at, and a credit note against one of them
reverses at the old rate.
Where an authority restricts what may appear on a statutory document, the permitted tax set is configured per country per template, and the designer
refuses a line it does not allow.
Every tax line states the account it posts to, so the printed document and the ledger entry come from one configuration and cannot drift apart.
