r6:  | 1. Chart of accounts | ANSWERED IN FULL — tab "Chart of accounts". This is the standard chart the system is to be delivered with, built to the structure mandated in 2.2.1 of the specification: six-digit fixed-length numeric codes, class taken from the first digit, a group hierarchy that never accepts postings, control accounts flagged, and every account mapped to a statement line, a note and a cash flow classification. Load this as the delivered default. It is not an entity-specific chart: any entity may adopt it as it stands, adopt it and amend it, or upload its own, each route passing the approval process in 2.2.2.
r8:  | 2. Monthly volumes | ANSWERED — tab "Volume and capacity". Recommended build targets across four scales, sized for a large multinational group, with a tested ceiling for each. Build against the "Multinational group" column. None of these figures is a constant in the code: every one is a configuration value an administrator raises in the system, with no release, redeployment or restart, recorded in the audit log.
r10:  | 3. Invoice formats | ANSWERED — tab "Invoice template library", with the layouts themselves in the companion file TIQACC-invoice-template-library.pdf. Fourteen templates are delivered with the system, covering Sri Lanka and the other jurisdictions in the group, plus the documents that run alongside an invoice. Tab "SL tax invoice field map" builds the Sri Lanka tax invoice field by field against Gazette 2481/22, including the full serial number rule. Entities may brand, amend or replace any template and may hold as many as they need.
r12:  | Reading the chart | Tab "Code structure" sets out the reserved code ranges, the meaning of every column, the account code segments and how the chart expands without renumbering. Read it before loading the chart.
r14:  | Nothing here is mandatory | Tab "Setup options" lists every option an administrator sets per entity, with the delivered default for four organisation profiles — from a single entity running the ledger alone to a multinational group. Control accounts, the entity and country segments in the account reference, the code allocation step and the approval requirements are all options, not fixed behaviour. A single entity sees account 122010; the same account in a multinational group reads LK-01-122010.
r16:  | Nothing here overrides the specification | Where this workbook and the requirement specification differ, the specification governs. This workbook supplies data; it does not vary a requirement.

===== SHEET Code structure =====
r2:  | How the chart is built
r4:  | Code format
r5:  | Length | Six digits, fixed length, numeric only. No letters, no separators, no alphanumeric segments.
r6:  | Digit 1 | Class. 1 asset, 2 liability, 3 equity, 4 income, 5 expense.
r7:  | Digits 1–2 | Level 2 group within the class, for example 11 non-current assets, 12 current assets.
r8:  | Digits 1–3 | Level 3 sub-group, for example 111 property, plant and equipment, 125 cash and cash equivalents.
r9:  | Digits 4–6 | The posting account within its group, allocated in tens so accounts can be inserted between existing ones without renumbering.
r10:  | Reserved endings | x9xx accounts carry provisions, allowances, accumulated depreciation and impairment, so a group and its contra accounts sit together and sort together.
r11:  | Dimensions | Never encoded into the code. Entity, branch, cost centre, department, project and location are dimensions on the posting, not digits in the account (specification 2.2.1).
r13:  | Reserved class ranges
r14:  | 100000 – 199999 | Assets. 110000 non-current, 120000 current.
r15:  | 200000 – 299999 | Liabilities. 210000 non-current, 220000 current.
r16:  | 300000 – 399999 | Equity.
r17:  | 400000 – 499999 | Income. 410000 revenue, 420000 other operating income, 430000 finance income.
r18:  | 500000 – 599999 | Expenses. 510000 cost of sales, 520000 employee costs, 530000 occupancy, 540000 administrative, 550000 selling and distribution, 560000 depreciation and impairment, 570000 finance costs, 580000 taxation.
r19:  | 690000 – 699999 | Unused and reserved. Available to an entity for accounts that do not fit the delivered structure, without disturbing the ranges above.
r21:  | What each column means
r22:  | Entity code | GROUP-STD marks this as the delivered standard chart rather than an entity chart. On adoption the entity code is replaced with the adopting entity, and the chart becomes that entity's own.
r23:  | Posting or group | Group rows exist for hierarchy and reporting roll-up only. The system is to reject any posting to a group row.
r24:  | Control account? | Yes marks an account whose balance is owned by a sub-ledger rather than by the general ledger. What that causes depends on the level set for the entity, shown in the last column and set on the Setup options tab: nothing at all, a warning and a recorded reason, or a manual journal refused outright.
r25:  | Control account type | Which sub-ledger owns it. The posting engine uses this to find the account to post to — an AR invoice hits the account flagged Receivables for that entity — so the value comes from a fixed list and is never free text.
r26:  | Statement line | The caption the account rolls into on the face of the statutory statements. Drives the statements without a report being edited.
r27:  | Note reference | The financial statement note the account belongs to. Note numbers follow a conventional running order and are renumbered to the entity's own note sequence on adoption.
r28:  | Cash flow classification | Operating, investing or financing, for the indirect cash flow statement. Cash accounts themselves carry "Cash and cash equivalents" because they are the closing balance the statement reconciles to, not a movement within it. Income and expense accounts carry "Not applicable".
r29:  | Default tax code | A placeholder mapped to the entity's own tax register on adoption (2.7.1). OUT-STD output tax at the standard rate · OUT-ZER zero-rated or export · OUT-EXE exempt · IN-STD input tax recoverable at the standard rate · IN-EXE exempt or no input tax · IN-BLK input tax blocked from recovery · IN-OUT outside the scope. Blank means the account is not a tax-bearing account.
r30:  | Dim: columns | The dimension rule per dimension — mandatory, optional or blocked (2.2.1). Income and expense accounts demand cost centre, profit centre, department and location so the management reports and the CFO dashboard in 2.15 can be produced without estimation. Balance sheet accounts block them, except location where stock, cash and fixed assets are genuinely held in a place.
r31:  | Currency restriction | Bank and cash accounts are restricted to one currency each, so the account balance is the bank balance and revaluation under 2.2.9 has a single rate to apply. Every other account accepts any transaction currency.
r32:  | Reconciliation | Required flag and frequency feed the reconciliation schedule in 2.12. Control accounts and current balances are monthly; non-current balances quarterly; equity annually.
r33:  | Owner | Mandatory for balance sheet accounts before approval, and assigned by name or role at entity set-up. The delivered chart cannot know the person, so it carries a placeholder.
r35:  | Account code structure — what is configurable
r36:  | Natural account | The six-digit code in column B. It means the same thing in every entity in the group, which is what lets the consolidation add like to like without a mapping table. Length is set at implementation, between four and ten digits.
r37:  | Entity segment | Identifies whose balance it is. Switched on for group, consolidated and multinational operation and off for a single entity. Two to six characters, taken from the entity record.
r38:  | Country segment | The country the entity reports in, as the ISO two-letter code or a code of the group's own. Switched on where the group operates in more than one country.
r39:  | Order and separator | Which segments appear, in what order, and what separates them, are set by the administrator. LK-01-122010, 01.LK.122010 and 122010 are the same account presented three ways.
r40:  | Generated, never typed | Segments are composed by the system from the entity and country on the posting. A user types or selects the natural account only, and the segments cannot be edited on a transaction.
r41:  | Not a dimension store | Branch, cost centre, department, project and location never appear in the code in any configuration. They are dimensions on the posting, and the chart would otherwise multiply beyond use.
r42:  | Where it appears | The full reference prints on reports, documents, enquiries and extracts; the natural account alone is what the user keys and what the group chart holds.
r44:  | Expanding the chart without renumbering
r45:  | Gaps as delivered | Accounts are allocated in tens inside a sub-group, so nine codes sit free between any two neighbours. Sub-groups are allocated in thousands and leave whole blocks free inside each class.
r46:  | Insert between two accounts | The system proposes the next free code between the two accounts either side of where the new one belongs, so a new account sorts and reports in its proper place rather than at the end of the group.
r47:  | When a block fills | A new sub-group is opened in the free range of the same class. An account never moves to a different class to find space.
r48:  | Spare class range | 690000 to 699999 is unused and reserved for accounts that do not fit the delivered structure, without disturbing any range above.
r49:  | Widening the code | Where the length set at implementation proves too short, it is widened group wide as one approved operation: every existing code is re-padded, every reference updated, and the former code retained as an alias so historic documents still resolve. It is a controlled migration, not an everyday change, which is why the gap policy above exists.
r50:  | Never renumbered | No account is renumbered to make room. A code issued is a code kept, because every document, report and reconciliation already issued refers to it.
r52:  | Loading this chart
r53:  | Order | Load class roots first, then level 2 groups, then level 3 sub-groups, then posting accounts, so every account finds its parent.
r54:  | Validation | The import is to apply the validation in 2.2.1 to this file as to any other: code format and length, class against code range, every account under a group, no duplicate code or name within a group, statement line and note present, dimension rules stated.
r55:  | Approval | Adopting this chart is itself an approved act, recorded with the version adopted (2.2.2). Every later addition or amendment passes the same request, duplicate check, review and approval.
r56:  | Not locked | No account here is undeletable or unrenameable, and nothing in the software is to depend on a particular account code existing.
r58:  | Control accounts — the flag against the enforcement
r59:  | Columns J and K are the account's nature | They say the balance is owned by a sub-ledger, and which one. That does not change with the size of the organisation: trade receivables control is owned by the receivables ledger in a one-person company and in a fifty-entity group alike. 68 accounts in the delivered chart carry the flag.
r60:  | Enforcement is a separate setting | How strictly that ownership is enforced is configuration, set per control type per entity — Not applied, Advisory or Enforced. The tab "Setup options" carries the levels, what each does, and the delivered setting for each shape of organisation.
r61:  | Why it is optional | A single entity posting everything by journal has no sub-ledger for a control account to be controlled against, so enforcement would block work and protect nothing. Activating a module changes that, and the system then proposes the matching control type be raised rather than raising it silently.
r62:  | Which accounts may be flagged | The control types are a fixed list — receivables, payables, inventory, payroll, fixed assets, tax and bank — because each maps to a sub-ledger the system holds. An entity may add or remove the flag on an account within those types; it cannot invent a type with no sub-ledger behind it.
r63:  | Reconciliation does not follow the flag down | An account whose enforcement is lowered stays on the reconciliation schedule in 2.12, because the balance still needs proving. Columns V, W and X are set independently of the enforcement level.
r65:  | When a code is already taken
r66:  | No code is ever reused | An active, blocked, archived or deleted account all hold their code. A code on a pending request is reserved until that request is approved or withdrawn.
r67:  | How far the check reaches | It follows the chart the entity runs on. An entity managing its own financial data independently is checked within itself, and two such entities may each use 122010 for different accounts. An entity posting to a shared group chart is checked across every entity on that chart, because the consolidation adds balances by account and cannot do so if a code means two things. A single entity, and an entity running standalone modules, see only the entity check.
r68:  | Joining a group chart later | Every code that exists in both with a different meaning is reported before the move, and resolved by mapping or renumbering. Consolidation cannot start while a clash is unresolved.
r69:  | The refusal says what is in the way | The account holding the code, its name, its status, its entity, and who created it and when. There is no override, for any role: a reused code makes two different balances indistinguishable in history.
r70:  | Alternatives come with the refusal | Ranked, so the user accepts one rather than guessing again — the next free code after the account the new one belongs beside; the free code between two accounts where it must sit between them; the next free code in the sub-group where position does not matter; and, where the sub-group is full, the first code of a free sub-group in the same class, with the system saying the block is exhausted.
r71:  | Shown in position | Each option is displayed with the accounts either side of it, so the user can see where the new account would sort before choosing. No code is allocated silently in place of the one asked for, and no account is renumbered to free a code.
r72:  | Near duplicates matter more | A clash of codes is mechanical. A duplicate of purpose is the real risk, so the request is searched on name, on the words of the stated purpose, and on the statement line and note it maps to, and close matches are shown before it can be submitted. An identical name within a group is refused outright.
r73:  | In bulk | An uploaded chart is checked row by row before anything commits. Every clashing row is reported with the account holding the code and a proposed alternative on the same row, and the file is accepted in full or not at all.

===== SHEET Entity in the code =====
r2:  | Putting the entity inside the account code — what it costs
r3:  | Requested before committing to the change. Design A is what the chart does today: the entity is a segment composed around a natural account that is identical in every entity. Design B puts the entity inside the natural account itself, so each entity has its own code for the same thing. Both print the same reference on a report; they differ in what the group has to do to add them up.
r5:  | The same three balances, consolidated
r6:  |  | Design A — entity as a segment | Design B — entity inside the code
r7:  | Colombo entity, trade receivables | LK-01-122010 — natural account 122010 | 01122010
r8:  | Dubai entity, trade receivables | AE-02-122010 — natural account 122010 | 02122010
r9:  | Bengaluru entity, trade receivables | IN-03-122010 — natural account 122010 | 03122010
r10:  | How the group adds them | Sum by natural account. All three are 122010, so they add to one line with nothing in between | The three codes are different, so each must first be mapped to a group account 122010
r11:  | What has to exist for that to work | Nothing | A mapping table, one row per account per entity
r12:  | If a new account is added in one entity | It is the same natural account everywhere it is used, and consolidates on the day it is created | It consolidates only once someone adds its mapping row. Until then the balance falls out of the group statements, and nothing fails visibly
r13:  | Intercompany elimination | Matching pairs are identified by account identity | Matching pairs are identified by resolving two mappings first
r14:  | Translating to the group currency | Applied by natural account across every entity at once | Applied per entity code, then re-aggregated through the mapping
r15:  | Drill from a group figure to a transaction | Direct — the group line and the entity line carry the same account | Reverse the mapping to find which entity codes contributed
r16:  | Renaming an account group-wide | One change, effective everywhere | One change per entity, or the same account carries different names and statement lines in different entities
r18:  | What it does to the size of the chart
r19:  | Profile | Design A — account codes | Design B — account codes | Against the agreed target
r20:  | Single entity | 206 delivered, plus its own additions | 206 — identical, no entity to encode | Target 2,000. Both fit, and with one entity the two designs are the same thing
r21:  | Multi-entity group — 100 entities | 206 plus group extension accounts | 206 × 100 = 20,600 codes | Target 25,000. Design B fits on size alone, but every shared account is now held 100 times and carries 20,600 mapping rows
r22:  | Multinational — 2,000 entities | 206 plus group extension accounts | 206 × 2,000 = 412,000 codes | Target 250,000. Design B is over half again the target, for one group
r23:  | Tested ceiling — 10,000 entities | 206 plus group extension accounts | 206 × 10,000 = 2,060,000 codes | Ceiling 1,000,000. Design B is double the ceiling and cannot be configured at all
r24:  | Figures are the 206 posting accounts in the delivered chart multiplied by the entity counts on the "Volume and capacity" tab. Group extension accounts are additional under both designs and are not in the multiplication.
r26:  | What Design B was going to buy | A reference that identifies the entity on the face of every report, document, enquiry and extract, without a separate entity column. Entity-specific accounts without needing an extension concept. And compatibility with a legacy chart or a statutory regime that expects the entity in the code.
r27:  | Why that benefit is already yours | Design A already prints LK-01-122010 on reports, documents, enquiries and extracts — the switchable presentation on the "Setup options" tab. The reference a user reads is identical under both designs. The only difference is whether those leading characters are part of the natural account, which forces the mapping, or composed around it, which does not.
r28:  | Where a legacy chart genuinely has it | Import it. The upload route validates the file, reports that the entity is encoded in the code, and proposes the mapping to the segmented structure — it neither reshapes the chart silently nor refuses the file. The old code is retained as an alias so historic documents still resolve.
r29:  | If you still want Design B | It is configurable: set the natural account to eight digits and the leading two as the entity. Say the word and I will restructure the chart, the specification and the consolidation sections to match. The cost is the mapping table above, maintained for the life of the system, and the chart sizes in the second table. Nothing else in the design changes.
r30:  | The narrower thing that may be what you want | An entity that needs an account the group chart does not carry already creates it as an extension in the reserved range, mapped to a group account. That gives an entity-specific code where the account really is entity-specific, without making every shared account entity-specific too.

r43:  | Where a limit is reached | The system warns at a configurable percentage of the limit — 80 per cent by default — naming the figure, the current level and who may raise it. It does not fail silently, truncate a run or reject a posting without saying which limit was met.

r45:  | These are capacity figures, not what you pay for | Named users and concurrent users above are what the architecture must carry, not a subscription entitlement. Nothing else in this table is metered as a means of charging: entities, transactions, accounts, documents, storage and history are technical capacity and are never a licence cap. r46:  | Data is kept from go-live, not for a number of years | There is no age at which the system drops a period. Closed periods may move to a storage tier that costs less and answers more slowly, but they answer: no restore request, no reload, no call to the vendor. Nothing is ever deleted without an approved disposal instruction from the entity, and the one exception — an erasure required by a privacy law — is a controlled action, recorded, leaving the accounting record and its totals intact.

Beyond it, capacity is a matter of infrastructure, and the system is to scale horizontally rather than require a re-architecture.

r39:  | Why control accounts are optional | A single entity posting everything by journal has no sub-ledger for a control account to be controlled against, so enforcement would block work and protect nothing. The moment sub-ledgers are running, the GL balance is a summary of detail held elsewhere and the two must be kept equal — which is what enforcement does. Activating a module therefore proposes the matching control type be raised to Enforced, and says so rather than changing it silently.
r40:  | Why the group profiles are enforced | In a group the receivables control balance feeds consolidation, intercompany elimination and the statutory statements of more than one entity. A manual journal into it breaks the agreement between the ledger and the customer detail in a way that no reconciliation can attribute afterwards. Across currencies it is worse, because the difference is then also an unexplained translation movement.
r41:  | Why the entity and country sit outside the account | The natural account must mean the same thing in every entity, or the consolidation cannot add like to like without a mapping table that someone has to maintain. Putting the entity inside the code gives each entity its own private chart and makes group reporting a reconciliation exercise. Putting it in a segment around the code gives the same readable reference — LK-01-122010 — while the group chart stays single.
r42:  | What an entity sees | A single entity sees 122010 and nothing else. The same account in a multinational group reads LK-01-122010. It is one account, one meaning, one group chart, presented according to how the organisation is shaped.
