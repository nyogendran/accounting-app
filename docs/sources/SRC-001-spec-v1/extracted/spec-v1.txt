i TIQACC system overview
TIQACC is to be a cloud-hosted, web-based accounting system: a single centralised platform on which the entity records, processes
and reports every financial transaction. It is accessed through a browser from any location with no local installation, automates the
accounting workflow end to end, keeps financial data accurate and consistent across every module, maintains compliance with the
regulatory requirements of each jurisdiction the group operates in, and gives management real-time visibility of financial
performance.
TIQACC captures transactions from the business processes that generate them — sales, purchases, payments, receipts, payroll and
banking activity. Each transaction is validated, classified and auto-posted to the general ledger through predefined accounting rules;
adjustments can also be made by manual journal posting where required, under approval and with the supporting working attached.
From the general ledger the system produces the income statement, statement of financial position, statement of cash flows,
statement of changes in equity, trial balance and management reports. Every transaction carries a complete audit trail, supporting
transparency, compliance and audit requirements.

own, the outputs it must produce, and the acceptance tests it must pass before that module is signed off.

2.1 Platform, access and security
2.1.1 Deployment and access
1. Cloud-hosted, multi-tenant or single-tenant, accessed through a current browser with no local installation and no browser plug-in.
2. Three environments to be provided and kept in step: production, test or sandbox refreshed from production on request, and
training.
3. Mobile application, or a responsive mobile browser view, covering at minimum: approval of requisitions, purchase orders,
payments and leave; expense and bill photo capture; dashboard viewing.
4. Offline behaviour: the system must fail safe. A dropped connection during data entry must not create a partial or duplicate
transaction.
5. Session timeout after [30] minutes of inactivity, with unsaved work recoverable on re-login.
2.1.2 User roles and segregation of duties
Roles are built by the entity's administrator, not fixed by the vendor. One user may hold several roles, and the rules on which roles
may not be combined are maintained by the administrator to suit the company's own structure and headcount.
How a role is built
1. A role is a named set of granular permissions, each permission being a module, an object and an action — view, create, amend,
approve, post, cancel, export or administer. Permissions are not bundled into fixed packages that the entity cannot take apart.
2. Permissions can be scoped as well as granted: to one or more entities, locations, departments, cost centres, projects, bank
accounts or document types, so that the same role can mean different things in different parts of the business.
3. Value limits are part of the role — an approval permission carries the band it applies within, as set out in 2.5.5.
4. Roles can be created from scratch, copied from an existing role and adjusted, or built by the administrator to mirror a position in
the organisation chart.
5. Changes to a role take effect for every user holding it, are effective-dated, and are written to the audit log with before and after
values.
Users and multiple roles
1. A user may be assigned any number of roles. Their effective permissions are the union of those roles, except that an explicit denial
in any role overrides a grant in another.
2. Roles can be assigned per entity, so one person may be, for example, a preparer in one company and an approver in another,
within the same login.
3. Assignment is effective-dated, supports temporary assignment with an automatic end date, and supports delegation during
absence without sharing credentials. The delegate acts under their own login, and documents show that they acted as delegate
and for whom.
4. A user's effective permissions can be viewed on one screen — every permission they hold, and which role grants it — and exported
for audit.
5. Roles can be assigned in bulk by position, department or location, and a leaver is disabled in one action across every entity.

Segregation of duties, configured by the administrator
1. The administrator maintains a segregation of duties matrix: the pairs or groups of permissions that must not be held by the same
user. The matrix is the entity's own, and is editable without vendor involvement.
2. The system checks the matrix when roles are assigned and warns or blocks, as the administrator sets per rule, naming the
conflicting permissions rather than giving a generic refusal.
3. Where the company is too small for full separation, a conflict can be accepted as a documented exception: approved by [role],
given an expiry date, recorded with the compensating control relied on, and listed on a standing exceptions report.
4. A conflict report can be run at any time across all users and entities, and forms part of the audit pack in 2.11.6.
5. Two controls are enforced by the system itself and cannot be switched off by any role configuration: a user cannot approve a
document they raised, and no role can amend or delete the audit log.
6. Every other separation — including preparing and reviewing the same reconciliation, and closing a period and approving its
reopening — is enforced by default but can be released for a named user as a documented exception under the rule above, so that
a small finance team with one qualified person is not blocked from operating. A released separation is approved by [role], carries
an expiry date and a compensating control, appears on the standing exceptions report, and every transaction processed under it is
identifiable.

2.1.6 External system integration and connectivity
TIQACC is the accounting record of the group, and it is expected to sit alongside systems the entity already runs. Anything another
system produces that has an accounting consequence reaches the ledger through this interface, is validated and posted by the rules in
2.6.10, and is traceable back to the system that sent it. Nothing arrives in the ledger from outside without a record of where it came
from.
How systems connect
Method Requirement
Open REST API The primary route. Documented, versioned and published, covering every master record and
transaction type the system holds — at minimum customers, suppliers, items, employees, assets,
projects, dimensions, accounts, sales orders, invoices, receipts, purchase orders, goods receipts,
supplier invoices, payments, journals, stock movements, payroll results and balances
Web services For systems that cannot consume the API directly, with the same operations and the same controls
File import and export Excel, CSV, XML and JSON, against published templates and schemas, by upload or by collection from
a monitored location
Database connection A read interface for reporting and extraction tools, exposing a documented and stable view rather
than the physical tables, so a schema change in a release does not break it
Scheduled synchronisation Any interface can be run on a schedule, per interface and per entity, with its own calendar and retry
policy
Real-time synchronisation Event-driven, where the sending system calls as the transaction happens, or where TIQACC notifies a
subscriber that something has changed

Master data synchronisation
Record Requirement
Customers · suppliers · employees Created and amended in either system and kept in step, with the system of record stated per field
Inventory items · fixed assets · projects Code, description, category and status synchronised; financial attributes remain TIQACC's

1. One system is nominated as the system of record for each record type, and for individual fields where the two differ. The other
system receives, and does not overwrite, what it does not own.
2. A record created externally is matched to an existing record before a new one is created, on code, registration number or another
stated key, so synchronisation does not produce duplicates.
3. A record received for a code that does not exist is created only where the interface is configured to create; otherwise it is held as
an exception for a user to resolve.
4. A master record that a dual-authorisation rule governs — a customer, a supplier, a bank account — is created by an interface as a
pending record and takes effect only when authorised in TIQACC, per 2.3.1 and 2.5.1. An interface cannot be used to bypass an
approval.
5. Deletion is never synchronised. A record withdrawn externally is blocked in TIQACC, and its history is retained.
Controls on every interface

6. Approval workflows apply to integrated transactions exactly as to keyed ones. Where a transaction type requires approval, an
integrated instance is created in the state that requires it, not posted.
7. Reconciliation of each interface: counts and totals sent against counts and totals posted, per interface and per period, with any
difference itemised. A control report shows each interface's last successful run, volumes, failures and backlog.

2.1.9 Assistants and automated analysis
Several modules specify an assistant: a facility that examines the entity's own data and reports what it finds — exceptions, forecasts,
rankings, draft commentary. The rules below govern every one of them, and a module's own assistant section adds only what is
specific to that module.
1. An assistant reads data and produces information. It posts nothing, approves nothing, releases nothing and changes no master
record, rate, rule or limit. Everything it produces is a proposal that a named user acts on.
2. Every statement it makes names the figures behind it and opens the records concerned. An observation that cannot be traced to
the data is not made.
3. It decomposes and ranks movements; it does not assert a cause it cannot evidence. Where a movement is not explained by the
data, it says so rather than offering a narrative.
4. A score, probability or forecast states the factors and assumptions it was built from and is presented as an estimate, not as a fact.
5. Draft commentary is attributed to the system and is reviewed and approved by [role] before it is circulated, filed, or issued to a
board, an auditor or any other external party.
6. An assistant answers only within the access rights of the user who asked, exactly as a report would, and does not disclose through
a narrative what a report would withhold.
7. A finding about a transaction, a user or a counterparty is raised for review. It is never expressed as an accusation and never blocks
a transaction on its own.
8. Questions asked, answers given and findings raised are recorded under 2.1.4 with the user and the time.

2.2.6 Correcting an entry — before and after approval
Approval is the point at which an entry becomes a record of account. Before it, the entry is working material and can be changed.
After it, the entry is fixed: it cannot be edited and it cannot be deleted by any user, including an administrator. Corrections are made
by raising an adjustment or correction entry, which passes through the same approval as any other.

The approval event is the boundary. Everything before it can be changed; nothing after it can, and the trail covers both sides.

Before approval
1. A draft or pending entry can be edited by its preparer, or by another user with the same permission, as many times as needed. No
approval is required to change it, because it has not yet entered the accounts.
2. A pending entry affects no balance, no ageing, no stock valuation and no tax return, and does not appear in any report other than
a pending or exception listing.
3. An entry returned by an approver — rejected with a reason — goes back to this state and can be corrected and resubmitted.
4. An approver may amend a pending entry rather than return it, so that a minor correction does not require a round trip. The
amendment records the value before and after, the field changed, the user and the time, and the entry is flagged as approver-
amended on the journal and on a standing report.
5. An amendment by the approver beyond a configurable threshold — by value, by account, or by a change of entity or period —
returns the entry to the preparer for resubmission rather than being approved by the user who made it.
6. An amendment made by the preparer to a document already submitted for approval returns it to the start of the approval chain.
The approver route above is the only exception, and it is bounded by its own threshold.
After approval
1. An approved and posted entry is locked. No screen, import, API call or administrator action can change its value, account, date,
dimensions or party, and none can delete it.
2. A correction is made by a new entry — a reversal, an adjustment journal, a credit note or a debit note as the case requires —
which references the original document number, carries its own reason, and is approved by someone other than the person raising
it.
3. The correction is dated in the current open period, so figures already reported for a closed period are never restated behind the
reader's back.
4. The original and its correction are linked in both directions on screen, so opening either one shows the other.
5. Narrative, internal reference or dimension changes that alter no value and no account may be permitted after approval where the
entity chooses, under approval by [role], with the change recorded. This is set per entity and may be switched off entirely.

Matching
1. The system proposes the allocation automatically from the remittance advice, the payment reference, the invoice number, the

1. Part settlement of an invoice, leaving the correct residual.

3. An advance or credit balance applied to a future invoice.

2.4.6 Amendment, cancellation and reversal
1. An issued invoice cannot be edited — the statutory exception to the amendment route in 2.2.6. Value, quantity or tax corrections

marked cancelled, carrying the reason and the user.

value of the original document, and the cumulative value of all notes against one document cannot exceed it either; the system

4. Withholding tax is computed on the correct base per the supplier profile and the jurisdiction, deducted at payment, and reported
with its certificate.
5. Tax exemption on a supplier or an item is honoured and recorded with its evidence.

2.7.2 Entity tax profile
1. Each entity carries its own tax profile: which taxes apply, the registration number for each, the registration date, the filing
frequency, the currency of the return and the responsible officer.
2. The profile is built at company set-up from the entity's jurisdiction and its industry or business activity. Selecting the industry
proposes the tax types that activity is ordinarily liable for — for example a turnover levy that applies to some sectors and not
others — which Finance then confirms, adds to or removes. The proposal is a starting point, never a fixed list.
3. Industry-specific rates and thresholds, where the law sets them, are held against the entity's profile rather than globally, so two
entities in the same country on different rates are both correct.
4. An entity is only presented with, and only produces returns for, the taxes in its own profile. A tax applying in one country never
appears on another country's entity.
5. Exemptions are held explicitly — the exemption basis, the certificate, its effective dates and its expiry. The system suppresses the
tax while the exemption is in force, reports the exemption on the return where the law requires disclosure, and warns [role] in
advance of expiry.
6. Registration and de-registration are effective-dated, so periods before registration and after de-registration are excluded
automatically.
7. Adding a new jurisdiction, a new tax or a new rate is configuration performed by the entity, not a development request to the
vendor.

account and dimension with the asset-level detail retained and drillable.
2. Runs can be scheduled, and the schedule is part of the period-close checklist in 2.2.8.
3. The run is previewed before posting, showing the total charge, the movement against the prior period and any asset whose charge
changed, with the reason.
4. Where the entity requires it, the run is reviewed and approved before it posts; where it does not, it posts on completion. Which
applies is configurable per entity.
5. An asset fully depreciated stops charging at residual value without intervention and can never be taken below it, or below nil
where residual is nil.
6. A disposal part-way through a period charges depreciation to the date of disposal before the disposal entry is calculated.
7. A change in useful life, rate, residual value or method is applied prospectively over the remaining life from the effective date, and
never restates charges already posted.
8. An asset may override its category policy only with approval by [role], and an exception report lists every asset not following its
category policy.
9. A posted run can be reversed and recalculated only within an open period, by [role], with a reason, and the reversal and the
replacement are both retained.
10. A re-run of a posted closed period is blocked; a correction is made by amendment under 2.2.6 or by an adjusting journal with the
working attached.
11. Catch-up depreciation for an asset capitalised late is auto-calculated from the date in use and posted in the current open period,
identified separately on the run.
12. Future depreciation is forecastable for any horizon, for the existing register and including planned acquisitions from the capital
budget, per 2.8.11.
13. Every run is logged with who ran it, when, for which entity, period and book, the asset count and total charge, its approval, and any
asset it failed on; a failed or pending run is reported rather than left unnoticed.

2.8.7 Revaluation and impairment
1. Revaluation upward and downward is supported per asset or per class, from a fair value, a market value or an independent
valuation, with the valuer, the valuation date and the report attached.
2. A revaluation adjusts cost and accumulated depreciation on the basis the policy states, recalculates the remaining schedule over
the remaining life, and posts to the revaluation surplus — with a downward movement charged to profit or loss to the extent it
exceeds any surplus held on that asset.
3. Impairment testing is supported per asset or per cash-generating unit, with the recoverable amount, the basis and the working
attached.
4. Impairment is recognised against accumulated impairment, and depreciation continues on the impaired carrying amount over the
remaining life.
5. Impairment reversal is supported, limited to the carrying amount that would have been held had no impairment been recognised,
with the limit computed by the system rather than asserted.
6. Revaluation surplus and accumulated impairment are reported per asset, per class and in total, and the movement for the period is
reconciled.
7. Every revaluation and impairment posts its ledger entries automatically and is approved before it posts.
2.8.8 Lease accounting — right-of-use assets
Leases that give rise to a right-of-use asset are held in the same register and depreciated by the same mechanism, with the liability
maintained alongside.
1. A lease record holds the term, the payment schedule, the discount rate, options to extend or terminate, and the contract as an
attachment.
2. On commencement the system computes the lease liability as the present value of the payments and the right-of-use asset, and
posts both.
3. An amortisation schedule for the liability and a depreciation schedule for the asset are produced to the end of the term and shown
before confirmation.
4. Each period the system posts the depreciation of the right-of-use asset, the interest on the liability, and the reduction of the
liability on payment.
5. Re-measurement on a change in payments, index, rate or term recalculates the liability, adjusts the right-of-use asset and
reschedules both from the effective date.
6. Lease modifications — extension, reduction in scope, termination — are supported as dated events, with the resulting gain or loss
computed by the system.
7. Short-term and low-value exemptions are configurable, with those leases expensed and reported separately.
8. The lease liability is reported by maturity band for the disclosure note and for the cash commitment view in 2.15.3.

6. Group depreciation reporting is produced on the group book where one is configured, per 2.8.4.

2.8.16 Acceptance tests
1. Entering only category, cost and date in use produces a complete and correct schedule without any other entry, and the final
period lands exactly on residual value with no rounding remainder.
2. An asset bought mid-period is charged according to the category's start rule.
3. A change in useful life affects only future periods and never restates a posted charge.
4. The periodic run posts to the right accounts and dimensions with no manual journal, and the asset register total agrees to the
control accounts after every run.
5. One asset carries a financial and a tax schedule simultaneously, each on its own method and rate, and the difference between
them reports as the deferred tax base.
6. Capital work in progress is not depreciated, and commissioning starts the schedule from the commissioning date.
7. An asset transferred between cost centres charges to the new cost centre from the effective date with no value entry.

cap is computed by the system.
9. A lease produces its right-of-use asset and liability on commencement, posts depreciation and interest each period, and re-

10. A physical verification reports assets missing, unrecorded and in the wrong location, and no register adjustment posts without

11. A posted depreciation run cannot be re-run for a closed period, and a reversal within an open period retains both the reversal and
