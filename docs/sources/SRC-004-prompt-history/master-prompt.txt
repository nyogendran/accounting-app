Strategic Architecture Discovery and Requirements Analysis

Act as a distinguished Chief Enterprise Architect, accounting domain expert, AI platform architect, and technology strategist.

Your task is to deeply analyse the attached requirements document and determine the most compelling, feasible, and differentiated technical direction for the proposed accounting platform.

Objectives
Understand the complete business problem before proposing technology.
Identify functional requirements, non-functional requirements, implicit needs, constraints, dependencies, ambiguities, and missing requirements.
Map the requirements to business capabilities and architectural drivers.
Investigate whether an AI-native, dynamically extensible accounting platform would provide meaningful value.
Evaluate how administrators could create and extend accounting capabilities through governed AI agents.
Assess the potential value of financial ML for accounting recommendations, anomaly detection, forecasting, and intelligent reporting.
Evaluate CQRS, event-driven architecture, event sourcing, modular monoliths, microservices, and alternative patterns without assuming any is automatically appropriate.
Identify opportunities for defensible differentiation from existing accounting and financial platforms.
Recommend a practical target architecture and a phased strategy for delivering it.
Areas of Investigation
A. Requirements and business domain

Identify the business capabilities, users, workflows, accounting domains, integrations, scale expectations, security requirements, and operational constraints.

Distinguish documented facts from assumptions and recommendations.

Create traceable requirement identifiers and identify the architectural consequences of each major requirement.

B. Product and technical differentiation

Research relevant accounting platforms and emerging financial technologies when external research is available.

Determine which opportunities are existing market capabilities, potential improvements, genuine differentiation, or speculative ideas.

Do not invent competitor capabilities or claim knowledge of proprietary architectures.

C. Dynamic accounting framework

Evaluate the feasibility of a reusable accounting framework supporting configurable or extensible accounting components such as accounts payable, accounts receivable, fixed assets, revenue recognition, inventory, reconciliation, and industry-specific modules.

Determine which capabilities should be metadata-driven, declarative, generated through AI assistance, conventionally coded, or protected within a deterministic accounting kernel.

D. AI and financial ML

Evaluate AI-assisted component creation, transaction classification, reconciliation recommendations, anomaly detection, forecasting, and intelligent reporting.

Compare deterministic rules, LLMs, classical ML, time-series models, and hybrid approaches.

Identify data prerequisites, risks, expected business value, and appropriate human-approval boundaries.

E. Architecture and data

Assess domain-driven design, modular architecture, CQRS, event sourcing, messaging, relational persistence, analytical data platforms, workflow orchestration, and alternative architectural approaches.

Explain where each pattern adds value and where it introduces unnecessary complexity.

F. Security and financial integrity

Identify essential accounting invariants, auditability requirements, authorisation boundaries, segregation of duties, tenant isolation, AI governance, and operational resilience requirements.

Treat deterministic financial correctness as a non-negotiable architectural constraint.

Required Deliverables

Produce:

Executive summary.
Requirements analysis and traceability matrix.
Missing requirements, assumptions, and stakeholder questions.
Business capability and domain map.
Competitive differentiation analysis.
Architectural options comparison.
Recommended architectural vision and principles.
Initial logical architecture diagram.
Dynamic accounting framework feasibility assessment.
AI and ML opportunity prioritisation.
CQRS and event-driven architecture assessment.
Major risks, trade-offs, and unresolved decisions.
Recommended MVP scope and implementation phases.
A prioritised list of architecture topics that require detailed engineering in the next stage.
Constraints

Do not start by selecting a technology stack.

Do not produce a generic microservices architecture.

Do not assume AI should control financial posting or establish authoritative accounting policies.

Do not recommend ML without explaining why simpler alternatives are insufficient.

Do not overengineer the initial release.

Where information is missing, state assumptions and their consequences.

Final Output

Conclude with a defensible architectural recommendation, the rationale behind it, the major alternatives rejected, and an explicit list of the design questions that Prompt 2 must resolve.

This stage is strategic discovery. Do not attempt to fully specify every API, database schema, sequence diagram, or deployment topology. Those belong to the architecture deep-dive stage.
