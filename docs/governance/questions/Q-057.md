# Q-057: What happens at a capacity limit, and who may raise it

**Question.** When a tenant reaches a configured capacity figure, such as users, entities or journal
lines, does the system warn only or refuse? Who may raise the figure: the tenant's own administrator or
Tiqri as operator of the shared platform? Is a cap on named users a licence matter (2.16.5) and not a
capacity one?

**What SRC-007 says.**

- "No figure in this section is a licence cap, a tier boundary or a limit the software enforces against
  the entity that bought it" (p.134, S7-2.16.1-c33).
- "An entity that outgrows a delivered default raises it itself, as above: a configuration value with
  an effective date" (p.134, S7-2.16.1-c34).
- "An entity approaching the tested ceiling is told before it reaches it, not after" (p.134,
  S7-2.16.1-c35).
- "The figures in 2.16.1 are technical capacity, not commercial entitlement" (p.136, S7-2.16.5-c23).

**Why it matters.** GOV-SCOPE-INCR-001 v1 (CAP-06, EXC-21) counts named users and entities against a
configurable limit. Without an answer, one team would build a hard cap and another a warning, and the
acceptance criteria would be guesses. On a shared platform, raising a figure affects other tenants
(Q-038).

**Open.** The product behaviour and licensing are the product owner's. Platform capacity is the tech
lead's. Who may raise a figure is the security owner's. Raised from REV-01M61MRRKX52P8XHG3NZXSPCZJ
finding B3.
